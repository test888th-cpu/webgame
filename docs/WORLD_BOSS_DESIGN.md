# World bosses: design and architecture

The owner's requests (November 2026):
- three more world bosses for the three bands past the story, each stronger than the last,
  with new skills, new attacks, weapons, new effects, a lot of health, more players and
  their own arenas;
- world bosses that run on the game's clock, not the player's;
- prizes far better than anything else ("god items");
- armed world bosses, with weapons two to three times as durable;
- fixes for the reported bugs.

This document records what was built, how it is meant to grow into a real online system,
and the decisions that remain the owner's.

**The three new raid bosses** (bold) rise in level, players, health pool and XP, one after
another. The Founder's Engine outlasts the Iron Sovereign.

**Moves.** Each new boss has moves of its own: copies of story moves with bigger numbers
and the same wind-up telegraphs, so they stay readable, plus a new heavy for every phase.

**Skills.** Every world boss, like every stage boss and mini-boss, also has a skill set of
its own (5-7 for a world boss, ids `WB_<BOSS>_00n`, in `client/data/boss_skills.json`,
see `BossSkillRules`). Its finishers open only in later phases. When the boss falls or
escapes at the bell, its skills and any hazard still waiting are cleared.

**New hazard kinds.**
- **Quake:** the street splits across its whole depth at the hero's spot. The answer is a
  step along the street, not across it.
- **Rain:** a volley of small drops scattered around the hero.

**What every world boss shares:**
- Each is armed. Any weapon a boss holds lasts `progression.json` `boss_weapon_durability`
  (2.5) times its hits.
- Unlimited entries and attempts, no daily limit and no wait between attempts (the owner's
  rule; guard tests).
- Open to every hero from level 10 (`progression.json` `world_boss_entry_level`), whatever
  the story has reached: no story boss has to fall first. Surviving is the player's call
  (the owner's rule, November 2026).

**Where the data lives:** all of it is data in `client/data/`, generated by
`tools/content/level70_content.py` (the `WORLD_BOSSES` table), and tuned there.

## 2. Rewards: Godforged

- World boss prizes have their own grade, **Godforged** (`ItemRules`: budget ×2.2, against
  ×1.6 for Legendary). It replaces the earlier Mythic and is held only by world boss prizes.
- A Godforged piece is worth about 38% more than a Legendary of its level, and at least
  30% more than any other piece of its slot and level (`test_level70_gear`).
- Every world boss pays its own pieces, as tier rolls and kill rolls.
- The item card's "Where to get" names the boss (`ItemSources`).

## 3. How a round works today, and the bugs fixed

The game is single-player for now: a round's health pool is kept on the hero's save, sized
for the boss's `players`.

**The world's clock.**
- A round opens and closes on real time (`WorldBossRules.window(now)`).
- An attempt's clock is the round's closing time, not a tick count (`WorldBossRun.tick(...,
  now)`), so a browser tab asleep in the background stops nothing.

**At the bell.**
- The boss escapes: untouchable, its skills stop, it walks off the arena and is gone.
- The hero is left alone, and the result shows.

**After a kill.**
- The boss is down for the rest of its round ("Already defeated this round. It rises again
  in ...").
- Never again a fight against an empty health bar. It rises next round, for any number of
  tries.
- A pool fully dealt by an attempt cut short also keeps it down, though only a kill seen to
  the end pays.

**The result screen.**
- It is built once and stays pressable while the street goes on behind it.
- Before the fix it was rebuilt on every tick, which is why it could not be pressed.

## 4. Target architecture: world bosses on the game server

This is how famous online action RPGs run shared bosses, and how this code is laid out to
get there (ARCHITECTURE.md: authoritative zone servers are a headless export of `client/`,
milestone IL-7).

```
 players' clients ──input only──▶  zone server (headless Godot, client/sim)
        ▲                            │  one arena instance per channel
        │ state snapshots            │  BossMechanics, WorldBossRun, ZoneSim
        └────────────────────────────┤  damage, hits, enrage: decided here
                                     ▼
                         world boss service (backend/)
                         · the round's ONE health pool, shared by every channel
                         · contribution per player (dealt), kill, escape
                         · rewards rolled and granted here, never on a client
                         · PostgreSQL: rounds, contributions, grants (audit)
```

**Clients send input only.** Damage, the pool, the clock, the kill and the rewards are
decided on the server (CLAUDE.md: "never trust the client for damage, currency, items,
loot, XP"). This is why the shared pool is not faked here with clients reporting their own
damage.

**One pool, many channels.**
- An arena holds a playable crowd, for example 30.
- More players fill more instances of the same arena.
- Each instance subtracts from the round's single pool through the service, so everyone
  fights the same boss and sees the same health.

**The server's clock.** The round, the enrage, the escape and the boss's next rise all run
on server time. Nothing pauses because one player looks away.

**Rewards.**
- The existing rules carry over: personal tiers by your share of your part of the pool,
  plus the kill reward when the boss falls within its hour.
- A player knocked out keeps the damage they dealt and may go back in, any number of times
  (unchanged rules).

**Why the code is ready for it:**
- `client/sim` is pure and deterministic (`test_sim_purity`).
- `WorldBossRules.window` and `pool` are functions of time and data only.
- `WorldBossRun.tick` already takes `now`.

**What changes when the server lands:** the round's dealt and killed state moves from the
save (`profile.world_boss`) to the service. `WorldBossRun` then reads the pool's health from
the server instead of from its own `_remaining`.

**Not claimed here:** a player count or concurrency figure. That needs the server and load
tests.

## 5. Decision for the owner: the shared bosses in the single-player build

Until the server exists, one hero cannot empty a pool built for many players within its
hour, and only the kill pays. Measured with `WorldBossCheck` (one hero at the boss's level,
boss gear +10):

| Boss | Players | Minutes for one hero to empty its pool | Round |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slag Titan | 5 | about 41 | 60 |
| Iron Sovereign | 10 | 85-97 | 60 |
| Floodgate Marshal | 15 | about 177 | 60 |
| Crucible King | 20 | about 191 | 60 |
| Founder's Engine | 30 | about 424 | 60 |

(2-3 seeds, 2-3 minute samples; no knock-outs with unlimited food.)

So the prizes of every boss built for 10 or more players cannot drop yet. The options all
change approved world boss rules, so none is applied:

1. **Keep it:** the raid bosses wait for the server (§4). This is the honest MMO answer, but
   their prizes stay out of reach until then.
2. **City crews (recommended for the interim):** while the game is single-player, the
   round's pool also falls on the world's clock, as if the city's other fighters were in
   the fight. That is a fixed, time-based drain (for example, enough for 80-90% of the pool
   by the end of the hour). The player's damage decides whether it falls in time and which
   personal tiers they reach. When the server lands, real players replace the crews. It
   fits the server's clock model in §4.
3. **Size the pool for the players present** (one, offline): every raid boss becomes
   soloable, and "more players" only means more health once online.
4. **Pay personal tiers without a kill:** full damage pays its tier rolls even if the boss
   escapes.

## 6. Effects: the plan and what blocks it

**Today.**
- Boss skills are drawn in code (`HazardView`): a container falling for the crane, a beam
  electric fields for shock, round vents for steam and sludge. The lane-wide laser
  (a thin red line whose reach read poorly) was retired: every boss that used it now strikes
  a chain of marked electric fields along the hero's depth, a row of sludge geysers or a
  quake, each showing exactly where it lands. Landings play their sprite effect across the
  whole area (lightning across a shocked field).
- Their landing bursts are sprite effects from **pixelfx** (MIT; its particle simulation
  adapts **Sprite Fusion's pixel art VFX by Hugo Duprez, MIT**). They are credited in the
  asset manifest and the game's credits with the full licence text.
- The new quake and rain are drawn the same way and land with pixelfx bursts.

**Next: proper sprite effects for every skill and its warning.** pixelfx has 266 MIT
effects, many adapted from Sprite Fusion's. The proposed mapping (`art/pixelfx/effects.json`
gains these ids, `tools/assets/import_pixelfx.py` imports them, and credits follow
automatically):

| Skill | Warning (telegraph) | Landing |
|---|---|---|
| crane | `scifi/scanner` (a red target) | `explosions/dirt-explosion` |
| steam | `water/bubbles` | `water/geyser` |
| sludge | `nature/toxic-puddle` | `nature/acid-splash` |
| shock | `lightning/electrified` | `lightning/zap-hit` |
| quake | `nature/earth-spike` (cracks) | `nature/quake` |
| rain | `fire/meteor` marks | `explosions/small-explosion` |
| enrage | `status/rage` on the boss | `explosions/implosion` |
| boss weapon swing | `melee/heavy-cleave` | `impacts/blunt-hit` |

**What blocks it.** Rendering an effect means running pixelfx's own renderer
(`node tools/render.mjs`, from the cloned repository at the pinned commit). In this session
the safety check stopped running code from an outside repository; the owner can allow it.

**Sprite Fusion's site.**
- `www.spritefusion.com` is blocked by this cloud environment's network policy.
- `claude mcp add ...` configures a Claude Code client on your own machine, not this cloud
  session.

pixelfx already carries Sprite Fusion's effects under MIT, so the site is not needed for
them.
